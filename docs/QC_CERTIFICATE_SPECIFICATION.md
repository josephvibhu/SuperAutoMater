# SuperAutoMater QC Certificate Specification & Audit Disclosures

This specification defines the audit taxonomy, customer-facing disclosures, and tamper-evident verification requirements for hardware certificates generated by SuperAutoMater.

---

## 1. Audit Data Taxonomy

To prevent misleading customer representations, all technical data presented on the SuperAutoMater QC Certificate is strictly categorized into one of three data domains:

### A. Measured Sensor Telemetry [LIVE PROBES]
Data actively acquired and verified during the physical QC session via direct hardware interrogation, low-level bus commands, or algorithmic stress workloads.

*Examples:*
- **Storage SMART Health:** Percentage of remaining lifespan reported by NVMe / SATA controller attributes (`Critical Warning`, `Available Spare`, `Percentage Used`).
- **Storage Power-On Hours & TBW:** Cumulative lifetime bytes written and host read cycles.
- **Battery Health & Capacity:** Real-time milliwatt-hour charge metrics compared against manufacturer design rating, including voltage sag behavior under step-load stress.
- **Thermal Dissipation:** CPU core temperature response, thermal decay half-life, and throttling status under AVX-optimized multi-core stress.
- **Hardware Bus Verification:** PnP device connectivity, display pixel integrity, audio transduction sweep, and input matrix verification.

### B. Declared System Specifications [FIRMWARE / DMI]
Data read directly from SMBIOS tables, ACPI DSDT descriptors, or system firmware without runtime behavioral validation.

*Examples:*
- **Chassis Manufacturer & Model Name:** (e.g. `Lenovo ThinkPad P15 Gen 2`, `Dell Latitude 7420`).
- **System Serial Number:** Authoritative alphanumeric identifier assigned by OEM factory tooling.
- **BIOS / UEFI Revision:** Installed firmware release and build timestamp.
- **Processor Family Architecture:** Declared silicon branding (e.g. `11th Gen Intel Core i7-11850H`).
- **Factory Battery Design Rating:** Theoretical initial capacity as programmed into the battery fuel gauge IC.

### C. Audited Waivers & Overrides [OVERRIDDEN / N/A]
Subsystems that could not be verified automatically, were deemed non-applicable due to form factor, or were manually waived by an authorized technician or supervisor.

*Examples:*
- **Touchscreen / Digitizer:** Labeled `[N/A - Non-Applicable]` on non-touch panels.
- **Cosmetic Blems:** Known scuffs, scratches, or minor keyboard wear accepted under customer agreement or grade tiers.
- **Station Anomalies:** Acoustic or optical tests waived due to bench environment noise, accompanied by explicit technician justification.

## 3. Cryptographic Verification Hash Algorithm

The verification hash is calculated using SHA-256 over a canonical, delimiter-separated representation of the run's aggregate data:

$$\text{Payload} = \text{RunId} \parallel \text{AssetId} \parallel \text{Serial} \parallel \text{PolicyVersion} \parallel \text{Technician} \parallel \text{Station} \parallel \text{Grade} \parallel \text{Results} \parallel \text{Overrides}$$

Where:
- $\text{Results}$ is an alphabetically sorted list of `Key:Status:OverrideReason;`
- $\text{Overrides}$ is a chronologically sorted list of `Key:Actor:Reason:Approver;`
- Delimiters ($\parallel$) ensure unambiguous field boundaries.

Modifying even a single character (e.g. changing Battery Health from 78% to 100%, or altering the assigned Grade) produces a completely mismatched hash, instantly exposing tampered documents.
